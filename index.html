Data & methods

Three stations · Strict 800 m network distance · One summer evening scenario

    Property routes and sun exposure

    Each property has one shortest route to the nearest entrance among the three stations, chosen across its available network access points. Access connectors are excluded from the 800 m network-distance budget. The property centroid may be separated from its network access point.

    Sun-exposed length sums the existing member-link lengths with less than 50% three-hour average shade along the parts traversed. All current routes run between network nodes and traverse complete source edges; their clipped reachable members were checked for full coverage. Longest continuous sun exposure is sampled along the ordered, joined path at intervals of at most 2 m; it is an approximate length between consecutive exposed samples. Priority-street lists show only the top 100 existing streets, ordered by their unchanged ranks, with the distance walked within each street.

    Entrance numbers are taken from the entrance records. Southern Cross repeats numbers across different approach streets, so the entrance street or location is also shown. These labels are not independently verified against station signage.

    Managing authority and street names

    The network uses City of Melbourne pedestrian asset records (including MCCID). City of Melbourne is shown as the managing authority. Under the Victorian Road Management Act 2004, the Code of Practice for Operational Responsibility for Public Roads, clause 10(3), councils manage footpaths and nature strips, including vegetation, outside the through-carriageway kerbs of urban arterial roads. Arcades, crossings and bridge decks carry separate notices in the street details.

    Street names are spatially matched to OpenStreetMap road centrelines, not official City of Melbourne asset names. Check street signs on site. Named roads are downloaded within the study area plus a 100 m buffer. Each member midpoint is matched to the nearest named road within 25 m; names are aggregated by clipped member length. If the leading name accounts for less than 50% of total length, the two leading names are shown. Unmatched streets remain unnamed. Cross-street extents are shown only where identifiable near both endpoints.

    Feels-like temperature and heat stress

    Feels-like temperature (UTCI, Universal Thermal Climate Index) describes thermal conditions at the selected time. Temperatures are reported as UTCI; categories describe heat stress.

    Method: SOLWEIG calculates mean radiant temperature at 13 times, every 15 minutes from 16:00 to 19:00 on 27 January 2026. Air temperature, humidity and wind speed are then used to calculate UTCI. MRT means mean radiant temperature, not feels-like temperature; the interface displays UTCI rather than MRT.

    Results: Median UTCI at sun-exposed points is approximately 45–51°C over the window, with extreme heat stress from 16:00 to 18:30. Median UTCI in shade is approximately 39–44°C, consistently very strong heat stress. Shade reduces typical UTCI by about 6–8°C but does not remove very strong heat stress in this scenario.

    Time variation: At 18:30, 93.79% of sun-exposed points reach 46°C; this falls to 24.42% at 18:45 and 0.057% at 19:00. Sun exposure is therefore a useful approximation for extreme heat stress earlier in the window, not a guarantee for every point or time. After 18:30, both group medians indicate very strong heat stress; local conditions may still be hotter.

    Ranking: The top 20 streets are identical when ranked using time-specific heat stress instead of the existing shade method. The existing shade ranking is retained. UTCI is for reference only and does not affect ranks, diagnostic categories or scores.

    Limitations: Weather is uniform across the area (CoM sensors 05 and 09). Wind speed is assumed to represent 10 m height; skies are assumed clear; surface materials are not differentiated; tree height is uniformly assumed to be 8 m. UTCI uses a 0.5 m/s wind-speed floor at 16:30 and 17:45; extrapolation using the original speeds does not change candidate ordering. This is a model consistency check, not validation against measured thermal conditions.

    Street details show separate median UTCI values for sun-exposed and shaded sample points at the selected time. Groups without samples are omitted, rather than combining hot and cool locations into a single street average.

    3D buildings use component elevations relative to the lowest elevation of each structure; actual terrain relief is not shown. Bridge decks remain flagged; differences in pedestrian elevation are an analysis limitation.

    Diagnostic thresholds: unshaded-position fraction 0.30; additional tree-shade contribution 0.10. Labels describe modelled shade sources, not the presence or absence of real trees or the feasibility of planting.

    Ranking score = Σ(member-link unshaded length × model route count). Street route counts are length-weighted means, displayed as approximate whole numbers. Excel exports retain the original decimal values and the member-link count (member_count), which records how many original network links were combined into each street. The slider changes the displayed time, not the three-hour ranking.
